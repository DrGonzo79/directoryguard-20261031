# Source notes

- **Report:** “Platform that monitors insurance directories and files correction requests for solo therapists” — October 31, 2026; public URL: https://www.ideabrowser.com/hub/ideas/today
- **IdeaBrowser metrics:** IB Index 6.5; service; $4M–$6M ARR ceiling; $95K–$120K year-one illustration; moderate difficulty; $5K–$20K capital; first revenue 60–90 days.
- **Buyer:** US solo therapists, psychologists, and 2–10 clinician mental-health practices accepting commercial insurance. Reachable market estimate in the report: roughly 115K–120K private-practice psychotherapists accepting at least one plan.
- **Painful status quo:** clinicians discover stale phone, address, or availability records through misdirected calls, then chase each insurer separately without reliable status. The report cites 81% directory-entry error prevalence and persistent corrections, while noting those figures do not prove therapists will pay.
- **Wedge:** scheduled multi-insurer checks, field-level evidence, filed correction requests, and per-plan status in one place. This prototype demonstrates scan → review → draft case, with mandatory human approval.
- **Why now:** 2026 federal/state activity increases 90-day verification pressure. Report examples include the REAL Health Providers Act, proposed CMS-4214-P, New York’s EmblemHealth settlement, Louisiana enforcement, California AB 280, and a shared industry accuracy standard. Regulatory details require legal verification before marketing.
- **Competitors/status quo:** Quest Analytics BetterDoctor (payer-side incumbent), DataSpring/CAQH, Headway, Alma, Grow Therapy, TheraProfessional, Credex, and Psychology Today. The real competitor is manual calls, portals, spreadsheets, and CAQH re-attestation.
- **Demand signals:** JAMA Health Forum secret-shopper study; Heard private-practice survey; Health Affairs Scholar market analysis; complaints in r/therapists and industry reporting. Exact buyer-language search volume is reportedly zero, making outbound/community distribution the likely path.
- **Offer ladder:** free ghost-listing scan; $249 first-month cleanup; $79/month per clinician Directory Guard; $790/year plus $29/month renewals add-on. These are report hypotheses, not validated prices.
- **GTM:** start with one state, license/state-association rosters, credentialing operators, therapist communities, and proof-rich free scans; avoid paid search where adjacent credentialing CPC was reported near $63.
- **Risks:** insurers may ignore corrections; free tools or credentialing incumbents can bundle the feature; correction access may violate portal terms; sensitive provider data and any PHI raise security/compliance obligations; low search intent means high-touch sales; ceiling is lifestyle-scale.
- **Build prompts:** phase 1—manual captures and reviewable correction packets; phase 2—consented insurer connectors plus case timelines; phase 3—90-day verification operations and aggregated accuracy benchmarking.
- **Source domains named in report:** JAMA Health Forum, CMS, defacto.health, crowell.com, Becker’s Payer, Louisiana Department of Health, Fierce Healthcare, Healthcare Finance News, Heard, Health Affairs Scholar, ProPublica, Seattle Times, and r/therapists. Claims were summarized from the live report, not independently re-verified here.
