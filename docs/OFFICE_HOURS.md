# Office Hours diagnostic

**Mode:** startup, pre-product. **Position:** do not build a broad monitoring platform first; sell a manually delivered correction outcome in one state.

1. **Demand reality:** evidence proves widespread directory inaccuracy, not willingness to pay. The next proof is five therapists paying a deposit after seeing a real scan.
2. **Status quo:** a solo therapist checks payer searches, calls support, files forms/letters, and tracks nothing consistently. Cost is admin time, reputation damage, and calls from patients they cannot serve.
3. **Desperate user:** an insurance-accepting solo therapist who left a panel/location, still receives misdirected calls, and has already failed to get a payer to fix the record.
4. **Narrowest paid wedge:** one-state, top-five-payer scan plus a human-filed correction packet and evidence/status tracker; $249 cleanup before recurring monitoring.
5. **Observation plan:** watch 10 clinicians verify a captured listing without coaching; record which fields they trust, what proof they can provide, and where filing stalls. Do not count surveys as validation.
6. **Future-fit:** 90-day verification may make recurring operations more valuable, but a national directory or payer self-cleanup could erase the scan moat. The durable asset would be correction outcomes and payer-specific resolution playbooks.

**Alternatives:** (A) manual concierge cleanup—S effort, low technical risk, recommended; (B) self-serve scan/tracker—M effort, medium risk; (C) insurer-integrated verification network—XL effort, high compliance/sales risk. **Assignment:** obtain five paid cleanup commitments from 25 evidence-backed scans before automating portal submission.
